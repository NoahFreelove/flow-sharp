<objective>
Bundle D — replace the three allocating `_source.Substring(_position).StartsWith(...)` probes
inside `SimpleLexer.SkipWhitespaceAndComments` with zero-alloc `ReadOnlySpan<char>` equivalents.

Today: every call site allocates a substring of the ENTIRE remaining source — kilobytes of
throwaway string per parse for files of any meaningful size, multiplied by the inner-loop
frequency of `SkipWhitespaceAndComments` (called once at the top of every `NextToken` and
also between every emit). For Flow files past a few hundred lines, this is the dominant
GC pressure source in the lexer hot path.

After: each probe walks a `ReadOnlySpan<char>` window over the existing string — zero
allocation, zero GC.

Purpose: Bundle D's contribution to the lexer/parser zero-alloc optimization series.
`bench_parse` is expected to drop the most. Builds on Bundles A+B+C (-8.8% to -31.8%
cumulative vs the 2026-05-24 baseline per STATE.md).

Output: surgical 3-site swap in `SimpleLexer.cs`, full test suite GREEN at Bundle C baseline,
`bench/results-bundle-d-<ts>.txt` artifact, and `260524-s2g-SUMMARY.md` with two-table
benchmark report (marginal vs Bundle C + cumulative vs original baseline).
</objective>

benchmarks indicate it.)

Replacement form — per .NET 10 idioms. Both forms below are valid:

Form A (lighter syntax — recommended):
  `if (_source.AsSpan(_position).StartsWith("Note:".AsSpan())) { ... }`

Form B (explicit ordinal):
  `if (_source.AsSpan(_position).StartsWith("Note:", StringComparison.Ordinal)) { ... }`

NOTE: `ReadOnlySpan<char>.StartsWith(ReadOnlySpan<char>)` (the Form A overload) IS
ordinal by definition — it's a byte-by-byte comparison. Form B is more explicit but
adds one parameter; either is fine. Recommend Form A for terseness consistent with
the existing one-line `else if (...)` chains.
